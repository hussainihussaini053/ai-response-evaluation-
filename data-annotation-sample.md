# Data Annotation Sample

## Purpose

This sample demonstrates my approach to data annotation, classification, consistency, and quality checking.

## Annotation Task

The task is to classify short statements according to their intended sentiment.

### Annotation Labels

- **Positive** – The statement expresses satisfaction, happiness, or approval.
- **Negative** – The statement expresses dissatisfaction, unhappiness, or disapproval.
- **Neutral** – The statement does not clearly express positive or negative sentiment.

## Sample Data

| Text | Label | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "I really enjoyed the service." | Positive | The speaker expresses satisfaction. |
| "The service was disappointing." | Negative | The speaker expresses dissatisfaction. |
| "The service was provided yesterday." | Neutral | The statement gives information without clear sentiment. |
| "I am very happy with the result." | Positive | The speaker expresses happiness. |
| "I would not recommend this service." | Negative | The speaker expresses a negative opinion. |

## Quality Checking

Before completing an annotation task, I check that:

1. The correct label has been applied.
2. The label matches the meaning of the text.
3. Similar examples are treated consistently.
4. No information is added based on personal assumptions.
5. The annotation guidelines are followed carefully.

## Handling Ambiguous Examples

If a statement is unclear or could reasonably receive more than one label, I would review the annotation guidelines and consider the full context before assigning a label. If the guidelines do not provide enough information, I would flag the example for clarification rather than making an unsupported assumption.
